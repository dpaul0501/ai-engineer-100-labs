Bars to scale. PageRank at α = 0.3 finds 2,292 of the 40,000 (user, future friend) pairs in its top 10s.
  • The walk is the best single score on this page: 0.0674 against 0.0524 for the count, +0.0150 (paired standard error 0.0015), and above Adamic–Adar's 0.0638.
  • It wins mostly by ranking, not by reaching. Of its 2,292 hits at α = 0.3, only 13 are people more than two steps away; at α = 0.15, which lets the walker wander further, 70; at α = 0.5, none. In a top 10, three-step people rarely beat two-step people. The gain comes from scoring the two-step candidates better: hubs diluted, plus credit for many short routes.
  • It costs much more. Each round touches all 160,000 list entries of the past graph, so 30 rounds are 4,800,000 updates per user, against about a thousand reads for the two-step walk. So walk scores are computed offline in batch, for everyone, and stored as each user's top list; section 9 shows how that runs across machines.

Trade-off and interview takeaway. Say: "random walk with restart; it sums all short routes with hubs diluted, its two-step part is resource allocation, it converges geometrically at rate 1 − α, and it runs offline." In an interview, also mention the cheaper estimate: run a few thousand actual walks and count where they stop.

In one line. Personalised PageRank scores everyone by a walker that keeps going home; it lifts recall@10 to 0.0674 here, mostly by ranking two-step candidates better, at a cost that makes it a batch job.
